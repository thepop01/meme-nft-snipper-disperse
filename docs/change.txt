I’ll validate the time-sensitive claims first, especially Robinhood Chain status, Solana Token-2022 behavior, and launchpad/LP mechanics. Then I’ll separate what is solid, what is heuristic rather than consensus, and what I would change in an implementable sniper pipeline.Broadly, yes. The architecture is right: **fast hard rejects, asynchronous soft signals, continuous post-entry risk monitoring, and separate Solana/EVM adapters**. The strongest parts are the emphasis on distribution, funding graphs, deployer history, bundle unwind, and exit logic.

I would change several details.

**1. Treat thresholds as hypotheses, not consensus**

Values such as `top-10 < 15–25%`, `bundled < 30%`, `MC/liquidity < 10–15x`, `>100 holders in 5 minutes`, and `buy/sell >70%` are useful starting points, but they are not portable across:

- bonding curve versus AMM launches
- token age
- launchpad
- SOL market regime
- pool depth
- token size and intended order size
- migration status

Store the raw measurements and calibrate thresholds using forward returns and survival outcomes. Do not encode only pass/fail decisions.

For example, `top10 = 22%` means something very different when:

- the top wallets are unrelated organic buyers
- one cluster owns 20%
- the bonding curve account is included
- the token has 30 holders versus 3,000 holders
- the top holders are already net sellers

Use adjusted metrics:

```text
top10_excluding_protocol_accounts
top10_cluster_adjusted
largest_independent_holder
largest_cluster
holder_concentration_velocity
```

**2. Correct the Solana authority model**

The mint and freeze checks are important, but “mint authority must be null” is not sufficient. Also inspect:

- authority history, not just current authority
- metadata update authority
- metadata mutability
- Token-2022 withheld-fee authority
- close authority where relevant
- default account state
- permanent delegate
- transfer hook program
- confidential transfer configuration
- account-level frozen state

Token-2022 extensions are not automatically malicious. They are capability flags. Some are legitimate for regulated or restricted assets, while permanent delegate, transfer hook, non-transferable, default-frozen, and nonzero transfer fee should normally be rejected for an unrestricted memecoin. Solana’s documentation confirms that these extensions can give a mint transfer hooks, global delegate powers, transfer fees, or frozen default accounts. ([solana.com](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/extensions?utm_source=openai))

The scanner should produce an explicit capability report rather than a single “Token-2022 = bad” flag.

**3. Separate bonding-curve risk from pool risk**

The liquidity section mixes two different launch models.

For a pump.fun-style bonding curve, the relevant checks are:

- curve progress
- virtual and real reserves
- creator allocation
- protocol migration destination
- migration state
- buyer concentration
- expected price impact
- post-migration liquidity depth

For a raw AMM pool, the relevant checks are:

- pool authority
- LP ownership
- withdrawability
- token transfer restrictions
- pool initialization provenance
- liquidity depth
- price impact
- whether the creator can add/remove liquidity

Pump.fun documents that graduation closes the bonding curve and atomically migrates liquidity to PumpSwap; the liquidity pool is protocol-owned rather than creator-owned. That is materially different from an arbitrary Raydium or Meteora pool. ([pump.fun](https://pump.fun/docs/bonding-curve?utm_source=openai))

I would not use “LP burned” as a universal requirement. Verify the actual pool program and whether liquidity can be withdrawn. A burned LP token is useful evidence, but the economic question is whether anyone capable of rugging can remove the liquidity.

**4. Do not treat “migrated” as inherently bullish**

Migration proves that the token reached the graduation condition. It does not prove:

- organic demand
- safe distribution
- sustainable liquidity
- absence of coordinated sellers
- that the post-migration pool is deep enough for your order

Migration should be a **state transition**, not a positive score by itself. Measure the first few post-migration blocks:

```text
post_migration_buy_pressure
migration_selloff
new_unique_buyers
price_impact_at_target_size
liquidity_depth
creator_and_bundle_wallet_netflow
```

A token that migrates and immediately loses 30% of its liquidity-adjusted price is not safer merely because it graduated.

**5. Replace raw “bundled percentage” with a wallet-cluster model**

This is one of the most important improvements.

A same-slot purchase is evidence of coordination, not proof of a harmful bundle. Conversely, a sophisticated operator can distribute buys across slots and funding paths.

Build a graph with:

- SOL transfers
- token transfers
- common funders
- wallet creation timing
- repeated transaction sizes
- shared programs and routes
- common first-funder ancestry
- synchronized buys and sells
- historical co-occurrence across launches

Then calculate:

```text
cluster_supply_pct
cluster_count
largest_cluster_pct
cluster_sell_pressure
funding_graph_depth
coordination_confidence
```

Also distinguish between:

- unsold coordinated supply
- coordinated supply already distributed
- coordinated wallets that are net sellers
- known independent sniper wallets

Jito bundles are atomic, sequential transaction groups and are submitted through a block-engine auction, so bundle-related analysis should distinguish actual bundle inclusion from ordinary same-slot activity. ([docs.jito.wtf](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnsend/?utm_source=openai))

**6. Add a mandatory execution-risk layer**

Your model evaluates whether a token is safe enough, but not whether your transaction can execute safely.

Add hard or near-hard checks for:

- quote freshness
- blockhash age
- transaction simulation
- account creation requirements
- compute-unit margin
- priority-fee budget
- expected landing probability
- maximum acceptable price movement between quote and landing
- failed-transaction rate
- RPC disagreement
- duplicate or stale pool state

The entry decision should be:

```text
expected_edge
- fees
- priority_fee
- slippage
- failure_cost
- adverse_selection
- exit_cost
```

not simply “score passed.”

For small pools, a token can pass every security filter and still have negative expected value because the round-trip cost is too high.

**7. Sell simulation needs stronger caveats**

A simulated sell is useful, but it is not a complete honeypot test.

On Solana, simulation may miss:

- state changes between simulation and landing
- insufficient pool liquidity after your buy
- account-specific restrictions
- dynamic transfer behavior
- route-specific failures
- token-account state changes

On EVM, a successful `eth_call` does not guarantee that the transaction will succeed after another transaction changes state. Re-simulating while holding is sensible, but pair it with:

- executable exit-size checks
- current pool reserves
- max-loss emergency exit
- allowance and approval monitoring
- tax-change monitoring
- owner/proxy/admin event monitoring

For EVM tokens, “owner renounced” should not be a primary safety signal. Renounced ownership can make a contract harder to administer but does not remove:

- proxy admin control
- hidden privileged roles
- external controller contracts
- arbitrary token taxes
- blacklist mappings
- trading gates
- malicious router behavior

Inspect the full privilege graph, including `AccessControl`, proxy slots, beacon contracts, timelocks, and external calls.

**8. Add a capability-based EVM scanner**

Your EVM section is directionally correct, but I would make the scanner inspect capabilities rather than function names. Function-name checks alone are easy to bypass.

Scan for:

```text
mint or supply expansion
balance manipulation
blacklist and whitelist state
trading pause
per-wallet and per-transaction limits
buy/sell tax mutation
fee recipient mutation
router mutation
owner/admin roles
proxy implementation upgrades
arbitrary external calls
hidden ETH/token withdrawals
anti-bot exemptions
```

Then test state transitions where possible:

```text
setSellTax(max)
blacklist(target)
disableTrading()
setMaxTx(0)
upgradeTo(malicious_impl)
```

Do not execute them on mainnet. Use bytecode analysis, fork simulation, and access-control inspection.

Robinhood Chain is no longer accurately described simply as “testnet/early rollout” if your reference point is today. Robinhood announced a public testnet on **February 10, 2026**, and its current documentation lists mainnet chain ID `4663`, testnet chain ID `46630`, and ETH as the native gas token. Treat mainnet availability and actual permissionless meme liquidity as separate questions. ([robinhood.com](https://robinhood.com/us/en/newsroom/robinhood-chain-launches-public-testnet/?utm_source=openai))

**9. Rework the score to avoid double-counting**

Several proposed features are correlated:

- top-10 concentration
- dev holdings
- bundle percentage
- sniper percentage
- fresh-wallet ratio
- holder age
- funding graph

Adding all of them linearly can over-weight one underlying phenomenon: coordinated ownership.

Use feature groups with caps:

```text
distribution:       max 30
developer:           max 20
liquidity/execution: max 25
flow/market action:  max 15
social/narrative:    max 10
```

Then apply penalties separately:

```text
hard risk penalty: contract capability
hard risk penalty: active coordinated selling
hard risk penalty: liquidity deterioration
hard risk penalty: deployer event
```

Do not allow strong social signals to compensate for dangerous contract or ownership behavior.

**10. Add survival and censoring methodology**

Your “runner rate” idea is right, but backtesting this market is easy to get wrong.

Track outcomes at fixed horizons:

```text
return_30s
return_2m
return_5m
return_15m
return_1h
return_24h
max_adverse_excursion
max_favorable_excursion
time_to_peak
survival_above_entry
```

Include realistic:

- quote-to-land delay
- failed transactions
- priority fees
- slippage
- partial fills
- inability to exit
- delisted or inactive tokens
- survivorship bias
- look-ahead bias from later holder labels
- API availability delays

Most importantly, evaluate each filter by **incremental PnL and calibration**, not only by rug reduction. Use walk-forward testing by date and market regime.

**11. Add a portfolio and operational risk layer**

A sniper system can be right on individual tokens and still lose through correlated exposure.

Add:

- daily loss limit
- per-launch exposure limit
- maximum simultaneous positions
- maximum exposure to one deployer cluster
- maximum exposure to one narrative
- cooldown after consecutive failures
- circuit breaker during RPC or network instability
- kill switch on abnormal sell failures
- separate hot-wallet limits
- deterministic transaction and decision logging

Also maintain a “no-trade” state when your data is stale or contradictory. Missing data should not silently become a neutral score.

**12. Suggested final decision pipeline**

I would structure it as:

```text
Stage 0: ingest
  program/account/slot events

Stage 1: deterministic hard rejects
  dangerous authorities/extensions
  impossible or invalid token state
  known blacklist
  invalid pool/route
  failed sell simulation
  excessive immediate price impact

Stage 2: fast ownership risk
  creator allocation
  cluster-adjusted concentration
  funding graph
  coordinated-buy confidence
  active bundle selling

Stage 3: execution quote
  target-size price impact
  round-trip costs
  landing probability
  minimum exit liquidity

Stage 4: soft score
  deployer prior
  organic buyer quality
  flow
  narrative
  regime

Stage 5: position sizing
  score plus uncertainty
  liquidity and volatility
  portfolio exposure
  expected value

Stage 6: continuous monitoring
  creator/cluster sells
  authority/admin changes
  liquidity changes
  sellability
  buyer/seller regime shift
```

The biggest conceptual correction is this:

> The system should not ask “Is this token safe?” It should ask “Given current state, target order size, execution costs, and exit conditions, is the expected risk-adjusted trade favorable?”

Your document already contains most of the right signals. The main improvements are to make them **chain/program-aware, cluster-adjusted, execution-aware, dynamically calibrated, and evaluated by realized PnL rather than static safety labels**.
